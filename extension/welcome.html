Still Recalled

Recalled baby gear is still for sale. Now the listing says so.

Still Recalled checks secondhand listings on eBay, Mercari and Craigslist against every U.S. CPSC recall since 1973, and flags infant sleep products linked to deaths. It needs nothing from you: open a search and badges appear.

Try it on practice listings Then pin it: the puzzle piece in Chrome’s toolbar, then the pin beside Still Recalled.

What it does

  1. 1

    Badges on the search page

    Every listing is checked as you scroll. A badge names what it found, like “Padded crib bumper” or “Possible recall”. Likely matches are red and possible matches amber, and the words always say which.

  2. 2

    A card that says why

    Click a badge for the recall, the hazard, which units are affected and the one question to ask the seller. Add a photo of the label and the model number is checked on this computer.

  3. 3

    A watch on what you buy

    Recall notices go to the first owner. Watch a listing and Still Recalled checks it against each new CPSC recall, twice a day, and notifies you if one matches.

Where the checking happens

On this computer

Every listing, by the word rules and Chrome’s built-in model, Gemini Nano. Only flagged listings go to the cloud: their title, description, price and photo. Your watch list stays here.

In the cloud

Only listings this computer flags or can’t settle, with the recall notice, so Gemini can confirm the match or rule it out through Firebase AI Logic. You can turn this off in the panel’s settings.

No match does not mean safe: it means no recall was found. Whether a unit is recalled usually depends on the model number on its label. Privacy · Source code
