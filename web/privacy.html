Still Recalled

Privacy

Updated November 25, 2026. This covers the Still Recalled Chrome extension, the web page at still-recalled.web.app and the Android app.

What stays on your computer

The extension reads the listings on Facebook Marketplace, eBay, Mercari and Craigslist search pages you open. On facebook.com it reads Marketplace pages only: never your feed, messages, profile, friends or groups. Every listing is first checked by word rules and, when your computer has it, by Chrome's built-in model (Gemini Nano), both on your computer. Listings these steps clear never leave it, with one exception: an infant sleep product that names no known brand (a "baby nest", a "lounger") is sent for a look at its photo even when your computer cleared it, because a generic title is how those are sold. Your searches and the rest of your browsing are not sent anywhere.

What is sent to the cloud, and why

When a listing is flagged on your computer, or your computer can't settle it (a bedding set that may hide a bumper, a possible part, or a product whose photo suggests a recalled brand its title doesn't name), its title, description, price and photo are sent, with the CPSC recall notice it might match, to Gemini through Google's Firebase AI Logic, so a larger model can confirm the match or rule it out. In our tests this was about one listing in four. On the web page and in the Android app, which check one listing at a time, the screenshot or text you add is read on your device when it has a built-in model, otherwise by Gemini in the cloud, and every baby product you check gets the cloud's look.

You can turn the cloud check off in the extension's side panel settings. A second setting, off unless you turn it on, sends every baby product on a page for the cloud's look, as the web page does. Google processes these requests under Firebase's data processing terms.

Yard-sale mode (Android app)

Yard-sale mode is off until you start it. While it runs, your phone's camera (about one frame a second) and microphone go to Gemini Live through Firebase AI Logic, so it can name the baby products in view and answer you. The recall list on your phone answers every lookup. A session stops on its own after a few minutes, or when you tap Done. Point it at the products, not at people.

What is not collected

  • No account to make. Each copy of the app signs in to Firebase anonymously: a random id, with no name, email or other personal information, used only so the shared recall guide below knows a real copy of the app wrote an entry.
  • No analytics, advertising or tracking, and no data sold or shared.
  • No personal information: names, addresses, payment details or messages.

What is stored

The extension keeps its results for each page in your browser for 7 days, so a page you return to isn't checked again, and keeps your settings. If you choose Watch for recalls on a listing, its title, price, photo address and link are kept in your browser so it can be checked against new recalls; you can stop watching it in the side panel. Removing the extension removes them. Your listings, photos, searches and watch list are not stored on a server.

The web page keeps each check you make in your browser (the result and a small copy of the picture), so you can open it again or download your log. It never leaves your browser. "Clear all" on the page, or clearing the site's data in your browser, removes it.

One thing is stored on a server, and it is about recalls, not about you: the shared recall guide. The first copy of the app to see a new CPSC recall stores the short summary Gemini wrote for it in Google's Cloud Firestore, with that copy's anonymous id, so other copies can use it without asking Gemini again.

New recalls

Twice a day the extension asks the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission's public recall API for recalls published since its list was built, and reads CPSC's public file of safety warnings. The requests contain no information about you. Gemini, through Firebase, reads each new recall notice (a public government document) to write a short summary used for matching, or uses the one another copy already wrote (see above). When CPSC's service can't be reached, the same public records are read from a copy of them on GitHub, through jsDelivr or GitHub's own file server. Your watch list is checked on your computer; a watched listing is sent to the cloud only if your computer flags it against a new recall, as described above.

Verification

To protect the project's model quota, Google's reCAPTCHA Enterprise and Firebase App Check confirm that requests come from this app. reCAPTCHA runs on still-recalled.web.app, including the small page the extension uses for this; Google's privacy policy applies to it.
